## Why it matters

Pathology Foundation Models (PFMs) are the modern equivalent of ImageNet pretraining for computational pathology — large image (or image+text/omics) encoders trained on millions of pathology patches/slides via self-supervised or contrastive objectives, transferring to many downstream tasks via frozen features or lightweight heads.

## Core idea

Pretrain a large encoder on a massive pathology corpus, then evaluate generality across many downstream tasks. The hypothesis is that pretraining scale (data, parameters, magnification) translates to clinical utility.

## Sub-branches

- **vision-only PFM** — UNI, GigaPath, Virchow, H-Optimus, CTransPath, Phikon
- **vision-language PFM** — CONCH, MUSK, PLIP
- **vision-omics PFM** — pairing pathology with bulk / spatial omics
- **slide-level pretraining** — GigaPath / Prov-GigaPath
- **multi-scale pretraining** — explicit cross-magnification objectives

## Key questions

- Patch-level vs slide-level pretraining objectives?
- Vision-only vs vision-language vs vision-omics?
- How does scale translate to clinical utility?
- What evaluation suite captures generality without overfitting to TCGA-like distributions?
